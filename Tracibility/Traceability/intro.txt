What Traceability Means in Automotive/ADAS
Requirements traceability is a structured method to track and link every requirement (especially safety-critical ones) 
throughout the entire development lifecycle — from initial specification, design, implementation, test, validation, and release artifacts. 

This is essential in automotive engineering to:
-->Ensure nothing is overlooked or mismatched across phases
-->Provide evidence for compliance with safety standards (ISO 26262, ASPICE, etc.)
-->Support verified implementation and auditability for validation/testing
-->Manage changes and impacts across the lifecycle 


Traceability normally covers:
Forward traceability — from high-level stakeholder and system requirements down to software/code and test cases
Backward traceability — from implementation and test results back up to the original requirements
Bidirectional traceability — full end-to-end linkage required for safety compliance and audits






ADAS consists of active safety, lane and cruise control, parking, driver monitoring, perception, planning, control, and safety mechanisms, 
all supported by diagnostics, communication, and rigorous validation.

🟢 L0 – No Automation (Driver Only)
👉 System may warn, but never controls the vehicle.
Typical Features (Warning-Only)
FCW – Forward Collision Warning
PCW – Pedestrian Collision Warning
LDW – Lane Departure Warning
BSD – Blind Spot Detection
RCTA – Rear Cross Traffic Alert
TSR – Traffic Sign Recognition (display only)
OVS – Over-Speed Warning
DDW – Driver Drowsiness Warning
📌 Driver performs all steering, braking, and acceleration.




🟡 L1 – Driver Assistance (Single-Function Control)
👉 System controls either steering OR speed, not both.
Typical Features
ACC – Adaptive Cruise Control (longitudinal only)
LKA – Lane Keep Assist (lateral only)
AEB – Automatic Emergency Braking
BSI – Blind Spot Intervention
PA – Park Assist (basic)
HBA / AHB – High Beam Assist
📌 Driver must monitor the system at all times.



🟠 L2 – Partial Automation (Steering + Speed)
👉 System controls both steering and acceleration/braking
👉 Driver must continuously supervise

Typical Features
ACC + LKA (combined)
TJA – Traffic Jam Assist
HWA – Highway Assist
LCA – Lane Centering Assist
ALC – Automatic Lane Change (driver-initiated)
S&G ACC – Stop & Go ACC
APA – Automatic Parking Assist
📌 Hands-on / eyes-on driver required.






🔵 L3 – Conditional Automation
👉 System drives on its own within a defined ODD
👉 Driver intervenes only when requested

Typical Features
TJP – Traffic Jam Pilot
AVP – Automated Valet Parking
NOP – Navigate on Pilot (limited ODD)
Highway Pilot (OEM-specific names)
📌 System is responsible until handover request.
